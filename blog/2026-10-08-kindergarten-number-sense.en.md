# Kindergarten number sense, now a full path of 16 skills

Kindergarten children can now follow a complete, ordered path through number sense: sixteen skills,
from saying the number words in order to the first steps of counting by tens. Each skill is short and
has its own small set of games, so a child always knows what they are working on and a parent can
see exactly where the next step is.

**What the path looks like.** It starts with counting: the number words up to 30, one word for each
object, and seeing a small group at a glance. Then come the ideas that make counting meaningful:
eight things are still eight when they are spread out, zero means nothing, and one group can have
more, fewer or the same. Next, children give exactly the number asked, match numerals to quantities,
and say one more or one less. The later skills count on and back, put numbers in order, tell which
of two numbers is bigger, split a number into two parts, use first to fifth, write numerals, and
try counting by tens, twos and fives. That last one is just for fun: it has no pass mark and never
holds a child back.

**New games where there were gaps.** Several skills used to stop at small numbers. Games now reach
20 where the skill calls for it, and about twenty new ones fill the holes: filling in the missing
number word in the teens, spotting a number made of dots on a die or fingers, grabbing a handful and
checking it by counting, a rocket countdown, lining up number cards, and a showdown between two
close numbers like 7 and 8. Every skill has two to four games, easiest first, so together they cover
the whole range.

**Clearer names.** Titles are short and descriptions use plain words, in English and in French
together, for example "Zero means none" or "One word per object".

Children's earlier practice carries over. Games that moved to a different skill keep their scores,
and each game now belongs to exactly one skill.
