Mathematics · Grade 5

  • Syllabus

Skills

  • Reading and writing numbers into the billions Reads and writes a number in words and digits with the right place groupings (8,431,000 is eight million four hundred thirty-one thousand).
  • Feeling how big a billion is Explains that a billion is a thousand millions by building it from stacked thousands and millions.
  • Comparing and ordering large numbers Orders 4,205,000, 4,250,000 and 420,500 by looking at the highest differing place.
  • Rounding large numbers in a story Rounds a number to a sensible place for the story (8,431,000 people is about 8 million).
  • Negative numbers: order and absolute value Orders −7, 2, −3, 0 and says that −4 is 4 away from zero (absolute value).
  • Adding and subtracting integers on a number line Works out −3 + 5 and 2 − 6 by hopping on a line.
  • One number line for every kind of number Places 0.7, ¾, −1.5 and 3 together on one line in the right spots.
  • Multiply and divide decimals by powers of ten Shifts digits to work out 4.7 × 100 = 470 and 3,500 ÷ 1000 = 3.5 and explains the shift.
  • Multiply and divide by 0.1 and 0.01 Sees that ÷ 0.1 acts like × 10 and × 0.01 like ÷ 100 (6 × 0.1 = 0.6).
  • Read and write powers of ten Writes 1,000 = 10³ and reads 10⁵ as 100,000.
  • Round decimals to a chosen place Rounds 3.476 to 1 d.p. (3.5) and to 2 d.p. (3.48).
  • Choose sensible rounding for a situation Chooses how far to round a length, a crowd or a recipe and says why.
  • Name digit values in decimals to thousandths Says what each digit of 3.472 is worth and writes 3.47 = 3 + 4/10 + 7/100.
  • Order and place decimals to thousandths Orders 0.305, 0.35 and 0.3 and places them on a number line zoomed to thousandths.
  • Multi-digit multiplication by a written method Multiplies 3,482 × 26 with the standard algorithm and checks by estimate.
  • Multiplying and dividing decimals by whole numbers Works out 3.45 × 6 and 7.2 ÷ 4 with a written method.
  • Decimal × decimal Uses scaling to see 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12 (3 × 4 = 12, then ÷ 100).
  • Long division by a 2-digit number Divides 925 ÷ 25 with a written method.
  • Expressing the remainder to fit the story Gives 50 ÷ 8 as 6 r 2, 6 ¼ or 6.25, whichever the story needs.
  • Decimal ÷ decimal (introduce) Scales both numbers by 10 so 1.2 ÷ 0.3 becomes 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
  • Order of operations Evaluates 2 + 3 × 4 = 14 and 18 − 6 ÷ 3 = 16.
  • Brackets and exponents in expressions Evaluates 2 + 3² × 4 and (2 + 3)² × 4 and sees the brackets change the answer; writes a bracketed expression for a story (3 × (4 + 5)).
  • Choosing a method Picks mental, written or calculator for 4,000 − 1,998 vs 6,284 ÷ 17 and checks against an estimate.
  • Column addition and subtraction of whole and decimal numbers Works out 12.5 + 3.482 and 20 − 7.35 in columns, lining up the decimal point.
  • Mental 2-digit × 1-digit Works out 48 × 6 by splitting into 40 × 6 + 8 × 6.
  • Multiples of 10 and 100 mentally Calculates 70 × 300 = 21,000 from 7 × 3.
  • Simple decimals mentally Calculates 0.6 + 0.7 = 1.3 and 2.5 × 4 = 10 in the head.
  • Deriving new facts from known ones Gets 4 × 0.3 = 1.2 from 4 × 3 = 12 and ⅜ of 24 from ⅛ of 24.
  • Common percentages of an amount Finds 50%, 10% and 1% of 360 in the head (180, 36, 3.6).
  • 25% and 75% by halving and quartering Finds 25% of 80 as half of half, and 75% as 3 quarters.
  • Estimating before calculating Rounds 4.8 × 52 to 5 × 50 and expects about 250 before working it out.
  • Spotting answers that are out by a factor of ten Knows that 4.8 × 52 cannot be 24.96 or 2,496 by checking the magnitude.
  • Common factors Lists the factors of 12 and 18 and picks out the common ones (1, 2, 3, 6).
  • Primes below 100 Lists the primes below 100 and tests a number for primality (is 91 prime? 7 × 13, no).
  • Prime factorisation (introduce) Builds a factor tree for 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.
  • Highest common factor and co-prime pairs Finds the HCF of 12 and 18 (6) and spots that 8 and 15 are co-prime.
  • Common multiples and lowest common multiple Finds that two buses leaving together meet again after 12 minutes (LCM of 4 and 6).
  • Divisibility tests for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10 Tells without dividing that 1,236 is divisible by 3 and 4 but not by 9.
  • Divisibility by 8 and 11 (introduce) Explores that 1,232 is divisible by 8 (last three digits) and 121 by 11 (alternating sum).
  • Square and cube numbers Knows squares to 12² and cubes to 5³ and reads ² and ³ notation.
  • Triangular, square and cube number patterns Sees 1, 3, 6, 10 grow by 2, 3, 4 and that two neighbouring triangular numbers add to a square.
  • Finding a common denominator Rewrites ⅔ and ¾ as 8/12 and 9/12.
  • Adding and subtracting unlike fractions Works out ⅔ + ¾ = 1 5/12 and ⅚ − ¼ = 7/12.
  • Adding and subtracting mixed numbers Works out 3 ½ − 1 ¾ = 1 ¾ by regrouping.
  • Comparing and ordering fractions above 1 Orders 7/4, 1 ½ and 5/3 and 2 ⅙.
  • Fraction as division Reads ¾ as 3 ÷ 4 and writes 5 ÷ 2 as 2 ½.
  • A fraction times a whole number Works out ⅗ × 20 = 12 and 4 × ⅔ = 2 ⅔.
  • Fraction × fraction with an area model Shades ⅔ of ¾ on a grid to see ⅔ × ¾ = ½ and knows the result is smaller than either.
  • Unit fraction ÷ whole, whole ÷ unit fraction Works out ⅓ ÷ 2 = ⅙ and 4 ÷ ⅓ = 12.
  • Fraction ÷ fraction (introduce) Sees ¾ ÷ ¼ = 3 and 4 ÷ ⅔ = 6 by counting how many fit.
  • Finding the whole from a part Works out ⅗ of ? = 30 is 50.
  • Simplifying fractions Simplifies 12/18 to ⅔ by dividing top and bottom by the common factor 6.
  • Converting fractions, decimals and percent Converts ⅛ = 0.125 = 12.5% by dividing, reads percent as "out of 100" on a 100-square (37% = 37/100 = 0.37) and moves between forms for common values.
  • Ordering a mix of fractions, decimals and percent Orders ⅗, 0.58 and 61% from smallest to largest.
  • Percent of an amount Finds 15% of 80 = 12 as 10% + 5%.
  • Percentage increase and decrease Works out a 25% discount on €40 (€30) and a 10% rise on 60 (66).
  • What percent one quantity is of another Says 12 of 48 is 25%.
  • Finding the whole from a percentage Works out that 45 is 30% of 150.
  • Ratio notation and simplifying Writes 6 red to 4 blue as 6:4 = 3:2 and writes a:b:c for three parts.
  • Sharing a quantity in a ratio Shares 20 in the ratio 3:1 (15 and 5).
  • Proportion by unit value and scaling Solves "3 cost 12, what do 7 cost?" (28).
  • Unit rates and speed Finds that 150 km in 3 h is 50 km/h and uses it to predict a distance.
  • Converting units by ratio reasoning Converts 72 km/h to 20 m/s and 250 cm to km.
  • Writing expressions with a letter Writes n + 5 for "5 more than a number" and 3 × n for "3 times a number".
  • Substituting into a formula Uses P = 2 × (l + w) with l = 7, w = 4 (22).
  • One-step equations and checking Solves n + 7 = 15 and 4 × n = 36 and checks by substituting back.
  • Simple two-step equations Solves 2n + 3 = 13 by undoing the steps (n = 5).
  • Pairs that satisfy an equation Finds pairs with a + b = 10 and a > b (9 and 1, 8 and 2, ...).
  • Stating a sequence rule by position Says "4 × position + 1" for 5, 9, 13, 17.
  • Using a rule to predict far terms Uses the rule to find the 50th term (201).
  • Two patterns from two rules Builds two sequences (add 2 from 0, add 4 from 0) and pairs the terms as (2, 4), (4, 8).
  • Relationships between paired patterns Sees each term in one pattern is twice its partner and says so.
  • Converting metric units with decimals Converts 2.35 km = 2,350 m and 450 ml = 0.45 l.
  • Choosing units and two-step conversions Chooses km, m or cm for a job and converts mm to m through cm.
  • Rough customary equivalents (non-gating) Knows an inch is about 2.5 cm, a foot about 30 cm, a mile about 1.6 km, a pound about 450 g and a pint about 570 ml.
  • Personal benchmarks Uses own hand span and pace to estimate a room (about 6 paces) and own height to estimate a door or a shelf.
  • Area of a parallelogram Finds the area of a parallelogram as base × height (6 × 4 = 24).
  • Area of a triangle Finds the area of a triangle as half of a base × height rectangle.
  • Area and perimeter of compound rectilinear shapes Finds the area and perimeter of an L-shape by splitting it into rectangles.
  • Volume of cuboids and compound cuboids Works out V = l × w × h in cm³ and m³ (4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cm³), including two blocks joined.
  • Volume and capacity: ml and cm³ Knows 1 ml = 1 cm³ and 1 l = 1,000 cm³ (a 10 cm cube holds 1 l).
  • Converting units of time Converts 3 weeks to days and 2 days to hours.
  • Decimal and mixed time units Converts 2.5 h = 150 min and 135 min = 2 h 15 min.
  • Reading timetables and finding durations Finds that a 08:45 bus arriving 10:20 takes 1 h 35 min.
  • Multi-leg journeys across midnight Plans a trip with two legs and a wait and an arrival after midnight.
  • Time in another time zone (introduce) Says that when it is 10:00 in Paris it is 09:00 in London.
  • Scheduling across time zones (introduce) Picks a call time that is a sensible hour in two cities.
  • Comparing offers by unit price Finds 2 for €3 (€1.50 each) is better than €1.75 each.
  • Prices after a percentage discount Works out a 20% discount on €15 (€12).
  • Staying within a budget Chooses items that total at most €20 after a discount.
  • Checking a receipt Finds the wrong line on a till receipt (bad discount, wrong unit price).
  • Shape hierarchy Says every square is a rectangle and every rectangle is a parallelogram.
  • Properties that define quadrilaterals Tells a kite, a rhombus and a trapezium apart by sides, parallels and diagonals.
  • Parts of a circle Names centre, radius, diameter and circumference.
  • Radius and diameter relationship Knows d = 2r, so a circle of radius 4 cm has diameter 8 cm.
  • Angles in a triangle Finds the missing angle in a triangle with 50° and 60° (70°).
  • Angles in a quadrilateral Finds the missing angle in a quadrilateral with 90°, 80° and 100° (90°).
  • Vertically opposite angles Knows that when two lines cross, opposite angles are equal (a 65° angle faces another 65°).
  • Drawing a shape from a specification Draws a triangle with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm using ruler, protractor and compasses.
  • Nets of solids Tells which unfolded shapes fold into a cube, cuboid, prism or pyramid.
  • Top, front and side views Matches a solid to its top, front and side views.
  • First-quadrant coordinates in real life Reads and plots a position such as (4, 7) on a map of a park.
  • Plotting in all four quadrants Plots (−3, 2) and (4, −5) and reads a point's coordinates.
  • Completing shapes with coordinates Finds the missing vertex of a rectangle (e.g. (1, 1), (4, 1), (4, 3) gives (1, 3)) and a point's mirror image.
  • Reading a scale on a map Uses 1 cm : 100 m to read that 4.5 cm is 450 m.
  • Using scale on plans Draws or reads a plan at a given scale (a 4 m room at 1 cm : 1 m is 4 cm).
  • Translating a shape Slides a shape 3 right and 2 up and states the move in coordinates.
  • Reflecting a shape Reflects a shape in the y-axis and in a given line and states the new coordinates.
  • Rotating a shape 90° or 180° (introduce) Turns a shape a quarter turn (90°) or a half-turn (180°) about a point and describes it.
  • Mode and range Finds mode 5 and range 7 in 2, 5, 5, 9, 3.
  • Mean Shares out to level the data and calculates the mean (3, 5, 7 gives 5).
  • When the mean misleads Spots that one extreme value drags the mean (1, 2, 2, 3, 40).
  • Median Finds the median of 4, 9, 2, 7, 5 by ordering (5).
  • Reading a pie chart Reads that a quarter-sized sector is 25% and ¼ of the total.
  • Drawing a pie chart (introduce) Turns 50%, 30%, 20% into a pie.
  • Line graphs with two series Reads two lines on one graph and estimates a value between two points.
  • Choosing the right graph Picks a bar chart, line graph or pie chart to suit the data.
  • Spotting a misleading graph Notices a truncated axis, a missing scale or a biased sample.
  • Language of chance and the 0–1 scale Places "unlikely" near 0.2 and "certain" at 1 on a scale.
  • Simple probabilities as fractions Writes the chance of a 5 on a die as 1/6 and a red marble from 3 red in 10 as 3/10.
  • Fair and unfair Says a spinner with unequal sectors is unfair and designs a fair one.
  • Experiments against predictions Predicts 25 heads in 50 tosses, runs it and compares.
  • More trials get closer to the prediction Sees that 1,000 rolls match the prediction better than 10 rolls.
  • Heuristics: work backwards and guess-check-improve Solves "I think of a number, double it, add 3, get 17" by working backwards.
  • Heuristics: simplify and look for a pattern Tries a smaller case to find a pattern before the big one.
  • Missing and surplus information Says which fact is missing or not needed in a story.
  • Finding your own mistake Finds the wrong step in a worked answer.
  • Always, sometimes, never and counterexamples Refutes "multiplying always makes bigger" with ½ × 6.
  • Multi-step fraction, decimal and percent problems Solves a 3-step story such as a shop trip and justifies each step.
  • Bar models Draws a bar model for "⅗ of the sweets are red, 12 are blue".
  • Is the answer reasonable? Rules out an answer by estimate or by the context.
  • Calculator judgment Enters a calculation correctly, checks it by estimate and knows when not to use it.
