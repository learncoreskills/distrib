Mathematics · Grade 2

  • Syllabus

Skills

  • Count within 1000 across hundreds Counts on and back across a hundred boundary (398, 399, 400, 401) and from any 3-digit start.
  • Read and write 3-digit numbers: digits, words, expanded Writes 352 as three hundred fifty-two and as 300 + 50 + 2, and reads either back to digits.
  • Compare and order 3-digit numbers Puts 407, 470, 74 in order and writes 407 < 470 using <, > and = by looking at the hundreds first.
  • Odd and even numbers Decides 17 is odd and 18 is even by pairing or the last digit, and writes an even number as a double (18 = 9 + 9).
  • Place a number on a 0–500–1000 line Puts 640 about right on a line marked 0, 500 and 1000 and reads a number off a marked point.
  • Skip-count in 2s, 5s, 10s and 3s from any start Counts forwards and backwards from 36 in 2s, or from 7 in 5s, and in 3s from any start.
  • Skip-count in 4s and 8s Counts in 4s from 36 (36, 40, 44…) and in 8s (8, 16, 24…), forwards and back (introduce-only).
  • Skip-count in 50s and 100s across hundreds Counts 150, 200, 250… and 340, 440, 540… from any 3-digit start.
  • Meet numbers to 10,000 Reads a 4-digit number built from blocks (2 thousands, 4 hundreds → 2400) and knows ten hundreds make a thousand.
  • Roman numerals I–XII on clock faces (local, non-gating) Reads a Roman clock face and says that IX is 9 and VII is 7.
  • Ten tens make a hundred; build 3-digit numbers Trades 10 tens for 1 hundred and builds 245 from blocks.
  • Value of each digit in 3-digit numbers Says the 7 in 372 is worth 70 and the 3 is worth 300.
  • Zero holds an empty place Tells 305 from 35 and 350, and writes "3 hundreds, 0 tens, 5 ones".
  • Partition a 2-digit number in more than one way Splits a 2-digit number as tens and ones in a non-standard way. Example: 43 is 3 tens and 13 ones.
  • Show a number in different tens-and-ones ways Shows 352 as 34 tens + 12 ones and as 2 hundreds + 15 tens + 2 ones, using blocks.
  • Add or subtract 10 in your head Knows 472 + 10 = 482 and 305 − 10 = 295, spotting that only the tens digit changes (except across a hundred).
  • Add or subtract 100 in your head Knows 472 + 100 = 572 and 905 − 100 = 805, only the hundreds digit changes.
  • Addition facts within 20 Recalls 8 + 7 = 15, 9 + 6 = 15 and every bond within 20 instantly.
  • Subtraction facts within 20 and fact families Recalls 15 − 9 = 6 and writes the family 6 + 9 = 15, 9 + 6 = 15, 15 − 6 = 9, 15 − 9 = 6.
  • Commutativity of +, not of −, and checking by inverse Knows 5 + 3 = 3 + 5 but 5 − 3 ≠ 3 − 5, and checks 52 − 17 = 35 by 35 + 17.
  • Add 2-digit numbers with regrouping Works 47 + 38 in a written method, carrying a ten.
  • Subtract 2-digit numbers with regrouping, then mixed fluency Works 64 − 37 by opening a ten, then mixes +/− within 100 without hesitation.
  • Add up to four 2-digit numbers Totals 23 + 41 + 18 + 7 by grouping tens and ones.
  • Add and subtract 3-digit numbers with base-ten models Shows 356 + 278 with blocks, trading 10 tens for a hundred, and draws it.
  • Column add and subtract 3-digit numbers, across zero Writes 356 + 278 and 403 − 168 in columns, regrouping across the zero.
  • Equal groups and arrays as repeated addition and × Writes 4 groups of 3 as 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 and as 4 × 3, and reads a 4-by-3 array as 4 × 3.
  • 2, 5 and 10 times tables with division facts Recalls 6 × 5 = 30 and 30 ÷ 5 = 6, 7 × 2 and 70 ÷ 10 instantly.
  • 3 and 4 times tables Recalls 3 × 7 = 21, 4 × 6 = 24 and their division facts.
  • Meet the 6, 7, 8 and 9 tables Explores 6 × 7 with arrays and the pattern of the 9s (9 × 4 = 36: digits add to 9). Not a gate.
  • ×0, ×1 and swapping factors Knows 7 × 0 = 0, 7 × 1 = 7 and that 3 × 5 = 5 × 3 but 12 ÷ 3 ≠ 3 ÷ 12. Subset of G3.MD.4: ×0, ×1 and commutativity only; associative and distributive strategies are not covered (introduce-only).
  • Multiply by tens and 2-digit × 1-digit Works 4 × 30 from 4 × 3, then 23 × 4 with a grid (20 × 4 + 3 × 4).
  • Sharing, grouping and thinking multiplication Tells "12 shared among 3" from "12 in groups of 3", writes ÷, solves 32 ÷ 8 by asking 8 × ? = 32.
  • Remainders and 2-digit ÷ 1-digit Says what is left over in 14 ÷ 4 = 3 remainder 2 and shares 72 into 3 equal groups with blocks.
  • Bridge through 10 when adding 1-digit numbers Adds 8 to 47 by 47 + 3 = 50, + 5 = 55.
  • Subtract a 1-digit number crossing a ten Works 52 − 6 as 52 − 2 = 50, − 4 = 46.
  • Add and subtract tens mentally Does 46 + 30 = 76 and 90 − 40 = 50 in their head.
  • Derived facts: scale a known fact Uses 3 + 7 = 10 to know 30 + 70 = 100, and 8 − 5 = 3 to know 80 − 50 = 30.
  • Mental ± of hundreds, tens and ones in 3-digit numbers Does 472 + 300 = 772, 472 + 20 = 492, 472 + 5 = 477 without writing.
  • Doubles to 50 Doubles 17 to 34 and 25 to 50 by doubling tens and ones.
  • Halves of even numbers to 100 Halves 86 to 43 by halving tens and ones.
  • Round to the nearest 10 on a number line Says 38 is nearer 40 than 30, and 35 rounds up.
  • Estimate a sum or difference by rounding Says 38 + 53 is about 90 and 83 − 41 is about 40.
  • Estimate a simple product by rounding Says 4 × 19 is about 80 by using 4 × 20 (introduce-only).
  • Check answers by estimating and by the inverse Checks 63 + 29 = 92 by estimating (60 + 30) and by 92 − 29.
  • Multiples as skip-counts Shades the multiples of 3, 4, 5 and 8 on a hundred chart and knows 24 is in the 3s and 4s, and counts how many numbers two tables share (introduce-only).
  • Patterns in the tables Notices that the 5s end in 0 or 5, even × any number is even, and the 10s end in 0 (introduce-only).
  • Unit fractions: 1/b of a whole Names 1/4 as one of 4 equal parts and 1/6 as one of 6, denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10; knows equal shares of identical wholes need not look alike.
  • Non-unit fractions a/b Shows 3/4 as 3 of 4 equal parts (3 pieces of 1/4).
  • Fractions on the 0–1 number line Places 3/4 and 3/8 on a 0–1 line cut into equal parts.
  • Fractions beyond 1 and whole numbers as fractions Knows 4/4 = 1, 3 = 3/1 and finds 5/4 just past 1.
  • Count in halves and quarters Counts ¼, ½, ¾, 1, 1¼… and ½, 1, 1½, 2… along a number line, forwards and back.
  • Equivalent fractions with models Shows with folding that 2/4 = 1/2 and 3/6 = 1/2. Models only, no rule.
  • Compare fractions with the same denominator Says 3/8 < 5/8 and explains "more eighths".
  • Compare fractions with the same numerator Says 1/3 > 1/5 and explains "smaller parts".
  • Count in tenths Counts 0.1 steps as 1/10, 2/10, 3/10… up to 10/10 and knows tenths come from dividing by 10.
  • Add like fractions within one whole (models only) Shows 2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7 with strips and says "the sevenths stay"; takes 2/7 from 5/7 to leave 3/7.
  • Find 1/4 and 3/4 of 20, and simple fractions of 6 and 12 Finds 1/4 of 20 = 5 by sharing in 4 groups and 3/4 = 15; finds 1/3 of 6 = 2, 1/4 of 12 = 3 and 3/4 of 12 = 9 with objects.
  • Missing number in + and − Finds ? in ? − 7 = 15 (22) and 8 + ? = 17 using the inverse.
  • Missing number in × Finds 4 × ? = 20 (5) and ? × 3 = 12.
  • Continue sequences in steps of 2, 3, 5, 10 Continues 7, 12, 17… and says the rule "add 5".
  • Sequences in steps of 100 and counting down Continues 850, 750, 650… and 500, 400…
  • Find the missing or broken term Spots 3, 6, 9, 13, 15 as wrong at 13 and states the rule.
  • Compare expressions without computing Decides 8 + 5 is more than 7 + 6? (equal 13: 8 + 5 = 7 + 6) by moving one across, without adding both.
  • Make both sides equal Fills 9 + 4 = 7 + ? and 12 − 5 = ? + 3.
  • Choose the tool and unit for length Picks a ruler for a pencil in cm and a tape for a room in m.
  • Measure to the nearest cm Lines up a pencil at 0 and reads 14 cm; with a metre stick or tape measures a room or door in m.
  • Estimate lengths and check Guesses 30 cm for a book, then measures.
  • How much longer Finds a 45 cm ribbon is 18 cm longer than a 27 cm one.
  • Lengths and differences on a number line Shows 12 cm and 20 cm on a line and the 8 cm gap.
  • Read kg and g scales Reads a scale to the nearest labelled mark: 2 kg, 500 g.
  • Read l and ml measures Reads a jug to the nearest labelled ml: 300 ml, 2 l.
  • Pick a sensible unit and the metric links Chooses kg for flour, ml for a spoon of medicine, and knows 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 l = 1000 ml.
  • Read a Celsius thermometer Reads 23 °C and says if it is hot or cold.
  • Tell time to the quarter hour Reads o'clock, half past, quarter past and quarter to.
  • Tell time to 5 minutes Reads 3:25 and 4:50 by counting in fives round the clock.
  • Write time and a.m./p.m. Writes 4:35 p.m. and says breakfast is a.m.
  • Time units: minutes, hours, days, weeks, months Knows 60 min, 24 h, 7 days, 12 months.
  • Days in each month Knows 30 days hath September and that February is the short one.
  • Duration in whole hours Says 3:00 to 5:00 is 2 hours.
  • Duration in half hours Works out 3:00 to 5:30 is 2 and a half hours.
  • Currency symbols and writing amounts (local) Writes 45c or 45p and a price with the right symbol.
  • Make an amount with different coins Makes 50 with 20 + 20 + 10 or 25 + 25.
  • Fewest coins Pays 65 with 50 + 10 + 5.
  • Totals of purchases within 100 Totals 45 + 30 for two items.
  • Change within 100 Works 100 − 65 = 35 by counting up from the price.
  • Sides and vertices of polygons Names triangle, quadrilateral, pentagon, hexagon, octagon from 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 sides and vertices.
  • Compare properties of 2D shapes Says a square and a rectangle both have 4 sides and 4 square corners.
  • Faces, edges and vertices of 3D shapes Counts 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices on a cube and names the square faces.
  • Sort shapes by properties Sorts 2D shapes into "has a square corner" and "has 5 sides" groups, then sorts 3D solids (flat faces, curved surface, can roll).
  • Draw shapes with given attributes Draws a 5-sided shape on a dot grid with a ruler.
  • Line of symmetry by folding Finds the vertical fold that makes two halves match.
  • Right angles Spots square corners with a paper set square.
  • Draw and measure a line segment Draws a 7 cm segment and measures a given one.
  • Are three points on a straight line? Lays a ruler to check whether A, B, C line up.
  • Rows and columns of squares Splits a 3-by-4 rectangle into 12 equal squares.
  • Quarter and half turns Turns a robot a quarter or a half turn.
  • Three-quarter turns, clockwise and anticlockwise Says "three-quarter turn anticlockwise".
  • Read a grid reference Finds cell B4.
  • Place and name positions on a grid Puts the flag on D2 and gives the reference of any object.
  • Write moves to reach a target Writes "right 3, up 2" to take a token to a target.
  • Routes with turns, and fixing a route Combines "forward 3, turn right, forward 2" and finds the faulty step.
  • Collect data with tallies Tallies in fives and counts the total.
  • Draw pictograms Draws a pictogram where 1 symbol is 1, 2, 5 or 10 and chooses the key.
  • Block and bar graphs, up to 4 categories Builds a bar graph with 4 bars on a labelled scale.
  • Read simple tables Reads a table of votes and finds the total.
  • Answer total, difference and "how many more" questions Reads that cats have 6 more votes than fish.
  • Plot measurements on a line plot Records lengths of 8 pencils as dots on a whole-unit line.
  • Run a survey and graph it Asks classmates, tallies and draws their own graph.
  • Certain and impossible Says "the sun rises" is certain and "a cat can fly" impossible.
  • Likely and unlikely Says drawing red from a bag with 8 red and 2 blue is likely.
  • List the outcomes of a coin or die Lists heads, tails and 1–6 as the possible outcomes.
  • One-step problems with + and − and the number sentence Solves "Sam has 28 stickers and gets 15 more" and writes 28 + 15 = 43.
  • One-step problems with × and ÷ Solves "4 bags of 5 apples" and "20 shared among 4".
  • Two-step add and subtract problems within 100 Solves "35 + 20 then give away 18".
  • Part-whole bar model Draws a bar with parts 18 and ? within 45.
  • Comparison bar model Draws two bars to show 12 more than 25.
  • Find all combinations Finds all 6 outfits from 2 shirts and 3 trousers.
  • Organised lists Lists outfits as shirt A with each trousers, then shirt B so none is missed.
  • Test a general statement Tests "odd + odd is always even" with five examples.
  • Check an answer by the inverse and by estimating Checks 72 − 38 = 34 with 34 + 38 and by about 70 − 40.
