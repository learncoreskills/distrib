Mathematics · Grade 4

  • Syllabus

Skills

  • Read and write numbers to the millions Reads and writes 3,070,400 as "three million, seventy thousand, four hundred" and back.
  • Compare and order numbers to the millions Decides that 4,206,090 is more than 4,196,999 by comparing from the highest place and orders a set of 4–5 such numbers.
  • Read billions by 3-digit groups (enrichment, non-gating) Reads 2,340,000,000 as "two billion, three hundred forty million" by reading each group of three.
  • Place big numbers on a number line Places 640,000 on a 0–1,000,000 line and finds the midpoint of 2,000,000 and 3,000,000 (the billions part is enrichment, non-gating; G4.NUM.1 covers only the to-1,000,000 part).
  • Roman numerals to M (local, non-gating) Reads XIV, XLIX and MCMXC and the year on a monument.
  • Lakh/crore commas (local, non-gating) Reads 12,50,000 as "twelve lakh fifty thousand" and knows it equals 1,250,000.
  • Count back through zero Counts 3, 1, −1, −3 on a number line and says what comes after −2 going up.
  • Gap between a negative and a positive number Finds the gap between −4 °C and 7 °C (11 degrees) or between floor −2 and floor 3 (5 floors).
  • Digit value in numbers to millions Says that the 4 in 3,482,150 is worth 400,000 and the 3 is worth 3,000,000.
  • Expanded form and the ten-times rule Writes 3,482,150 as 3,000,000 + 400,000 + … and says why the 8 is ten times the 8 one place to its right.
  • Decimal place value to hundredths Says that each digit in 2.71 is worth 2, 7/10 and 1/100, and writes 3.47 = 3 + 4/10 + 7/100 in expanded form. Thousandths are G5-PV-6.
  • Round whole numbers to any place Rounds 458,372 to 458,000 (nearest thousand) and 460,000 (nearest 10,000) and says why.
  • Multiply and divide whole numbers by 10, 100, 1000 Shifts digits to get 45 × 100 = 4500 and 4500 ÷ 1000 = 4.5.
  • Divide multiples of 10 and 100 by 10 and 100 Says 4,500 ÷ 100 = 45, the digits shifting right, as the undo of ×100. Decimal scaling is G5-PV-1.
  • Column addition and subtraction of multi-digit numbers Adds 4,687 + 2,759 and subtracts 5,003 − 2,768 in columns, regrouping and trading across zeros.
  • Check ± by estimate and inverse Checks 5,003 − 2,768 = 2,235 by rounding to 5,000 − 2,800 and by adding 2,235 + 2,768.
  • Add and subtract decimals in columns Lines up the decimal point to compute 12.4 + 3.85 and 7 − 2.35, and a 3-place case, 2.375 + 1.4.
  • Multiply 3- and 4-digit numbers by 1 digit Computes 4,237 × 6 with an area model and then in a column.
  • Introduce 4-digit × 2-digit Computes 1,246 × 23 as two partial-product rows and adds them (introduce only).
  • Multiply and divide a decimal by a whole number Gets 2.5 × 4 = 10, 3.2 × 3 = 9.6 and a hundredths case, 1.25 × 4 = 5, and shares 4.8 ÷ 4 = 1.2 (introduce only).
  • Divide up to 4 digits by 1 digit Computes 735 ÷ 5 and 4,284 ÷ 6 by sharing place by place (short division).
  • Decide what to do with a remainder Answers "58 children, 8 per bus" with 8 buses (round up) and "58 sweets, 8 per box" with 7 boxes (drop).
  • Introduce division by a 2-digit number Works 156 ÷ 12 by chunking with multiples of 12 (introduce, whole-number remainder only).
  • "Times as many" versus "more than" Says that 12 is "3 times as many as 4" but "8 more than 4".
  • Distributive and associative properties Calculates 25 × 4 × 7 as 100 × 7 and 6 × 98 as 6 × 100 − 6 × 2.
  • Order of operations without brackets Evaluates 3 + 4 × 2 = 11 and 20 − 12 ÷ 4 = 17, doing × ÷ before + −.
  • Brackets in expressions Shows that (3 + 4) × 2 = 14 and places brackets in 2 + 3 × 4 to make 20.
  • Multiply 2-digit by 2-digit Computes 34 × 27 with an area model (30 × 27 + 4 × 27), then in a column with two partial-product rows.
  • Multiply and divide multiples of 10 and 100 mentally Computes 30 × 40 = 1,200 and 2,400 ÷ 6 = 400 from the basic fact.
  • Add and subtract by rounding and compensating Computes 346 + 99 as 346 + 100 − 1 and 502 − 298 as 502 − 300 + 2.
  • Double and halve to simplify Turns 16 × 25 into 8 × 50 into 4 × 100 = 400.
  • Estimate products Estimates 48 × 6 as 50 × 6 = 300 and decides whether 288 is reasonable.
  • Estimate quotients with friendly numbers Estimates 412 ÷ 8 as 400 ÷ 8 = 50 using compatible numbers.
  • Choose the best mental strategy Picks compensation for 346 + 99, double-and-halve for 16 × 25 and tens-facts for 30 × 40.
  • Recall facts to 12×12 and matching divisions Recalls 7 × 8 = 56, 12 × 11 = 132 and 132 ÷ 12 = 11 instantly.
  • Find factor pairs to 100 Lists all factor pairs of 36: 1×36, 2×18, 3×12, 4×9, 6×6.
  • Recognise multiples Says that 56 is a multiple of 7 and lists the multiples of 9 up to 100.
  • Common factors of two numbers Finds that 12 and 18 share 1, 2, 3 and 6.
  • Prime or composite to 100 Decides that 47 is prime and 51 (3 × 17) is composite.
  • Divisibility rules for 2, 5, 10 Knows that 3,460 is divisible by 2, 5 and 10 and 1,375 only by 5.
  • Square numbers and ² Builds 7² = 49 from tiles and recognises 36 and 81 as squares; knows the squares up to 12².
  • Cube numbers and ³ Builds 3³ = 27 from blocks and reads 2³ as 2 × 2 × 2 = 8; knows the cubes up to 5³ (125).
  • Common multiples of two numbers (non-gating, G5 lead-in) Lists multiples of 4 and 6 and finds the shared ones: 12, 24.
  • Generate equivalent fractions Shows that 3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12 by multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number.
  • Simplify to lowest terms Reduces 12/18 to 2/3 by dividing top and bottom by the common factor 6.
  • Compare fractions with a benchmark Decides that 5/8 > 1/2 and 3/7 < 1/2 without a common denominator.
  • Compare and order with a common denominator Orders 3/4, 2/3 and 5/6 by rewriting them as twelfths.
  • Convert improper fractions and mixed numbers Writes 11/4 as 2¾ and 3½ as 7/2.
  • Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator Computes 5/8 + 7/8 = 12/8 = 1½ and 1 − 3/8 = 5/8.
  • Add and subtract related denominators Computes 2/3 + 1/6 = 4/6 + 1/6 = 5/6.
  • Add and subtract mixed numbers Computes 2¼ + 1¾ = 4 and 3½ − 1¾ = 1¾.
  • Decompose a fraction into a sum Writes 3/8 = 1/8 + 2/8 and 3/8 = 1/4 + 1/8.
  • Multiply a fraction by a whole number Computes 3 × 2/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5 as repeated addition.
  • Find a fraction of a quantity Finds 3/5 of 40 as 3 × (40 ÷ 5) = 24 in a word problem.
  • Compare decimals to 3 places Decides 0.45 > 0.405 by comparing place by place.
  • Order and place decimals on a number line Places 0.405, 0.45 and 0.5 on a line that zooms from tenths to thousandths.
  • Round decimals to the nearest whole Rounds 6.48 to 6 and 6.5 to 7.
  • Add and subtract decimals in money and measures Computes €4.75 + €2.60 and 3.4 m − 1.85 m in context.
  • Percent means "out of 100" Shades 25 squares of a 100-grid and says "25%, 25 out of 100".
  • Link percent, fraction and decimal Matches 25% = 25/100 = 0.25 = ¼ for ½, ¼, ¾, ⅕, tenths (7/10 = 0.7) and hundredths.
  • Continue a number pattern from its rule Continues 4, 7, 10, … from "add 3".
  • Notice what a rule does not say Spots that 4, 7, 10, 13 alternates even and odd although the rule only says "add 3".
  • Growing shape patterns Predicts how many matchsticks the 6th shape needs from the first four.
  • Use a symbol for one unknown Solves □ + 17 = 40 and 6 × □ = 54.
  • Unknown in a multi-step sentence Solves 3 × □ + 4 = 19 by undoing + 4 then × 3.
  • State the rule between two columns Reads the table 1→5, 2→10, 3→15 and says "multiply by 5".
  • Two-step column rules Finds that 1→5, 2→8, 3→11 follows "× 3, then + 2".
  • Metric length relationships Converts 3.5 km to 3,500 m and 450 cm to 4.5 m.
  • Metric mass and capacity relationships Converts 2.4 kg to 2,400 g and 750 ml to 0.75 l.
  • Estimate length, mass and capacity Estimates the length of a desk as about 120 cm and a bottle as about 500 ml, then checks.
  • Read scales with unlabelled divisions Reads a kitchen scale that goes up in 20 g steps with only 100 g labelled.
  • Solve measurement problems with the four operations Works out how many 35 cm ribbons come from 2.4 m, with 10 cm left over.
  • Area of a rectangle with a formula Computes 8 cm × 5 cm = 40 cm² and says why it is "squares in each row × rows".
  • Area of shapes made of rectangles Splits an L-shape into two rectangles and gets 12 + 8 = 20 cm².
  • Perimeter of rectangles and rectilinear shapes Gets 2 × (9 + 4) = 26 cm and walks round an L-shape to total its sides.
  • Find a missing side from perimeter or area Finds that a 40 cm² rectangle that is 8 cm long is 5 cm wide, and a missing side from a perimeter of 26 cm.
  • Same area, different perimeter Shows that 6 × 4 and 12 × 2 both have area 24 cm² but perimeters 20 and 28.
  • Convert 12-hour and 24-hour times Converts 3:45 pm to 15:45 and 00:20 to 12:20 am.
  • Read a timetable Finds the 14:25 bus from a station and its arrival time in a table.
  • Plan a journey with a timetable Chooses the train that gets you to school by 08:30 with a 10-minute walk.
  • Durations across the hour Works out 07:48 to 09:15 as 1 h 27 min using a time line.
  • Durations across midnight Works out 22:40 to 01:15 as 2 h 35 min.
  • Find a date weeks ahead or behind Finds the date 3 weeks after 14 March and the weekday it falls on.
  • Total and change with decimal amounts Adds €3.45 + €2.80 and finds the change from €10.
  • Multiply and divide with money Computes 4 × €2.45 = €9.80 and €7.80 ÷ 4 = €1.95 each.
  • Multi-step shopping problems Plans a party list: 3 pizzas, 2 drinks each for 6 guests, then the change from €50.
  • Estimate a bill and check the change Rounds €3.45 + €6.80 + €1.95 to about €12 and spots that change of €5 from €20 is wrong.
  • Points, lines, segments and rays Tells a line segment from a ray and a line and names them (AB, ray CD).
  • Parallel and perpendicular lines Finds the parallel and perpendicular pairs on a map or a letter shape and draws them.
  • Name angle types Calls 45° acute, 90° right, 120° obtuse, 180° straight and 250° reflex.
  • Measure and draw angles with a protractor Reads 65° from the correct scale and draws a 110° angle.
  • Angles on a straight line and around a point Finds the missing angle as 180° − 125° = 55° on a line and 360° − 270° = 90° around a point.
  • Classify triangles by sides Names a triangle with sides 5, 5 and 8 as isosceles.
  • Classify triangles by angles Names a triangle with a 100° angle obtuse and one with a 90° angle right.
  • Regular and irregular polygons Explains that a rhombus is irregular as its angles are not equal, while a square is regular.
  • Find lines of symmetry Finds all 4 lines of symmetry of a square and the single line of an isosceles triangle.
  • Complete a symmetric figure Draws the mirror half of a figure across a vertical, horizontal or diagonal line.
  • Parts of a circle and drawing with compasses Names centre, radius and diameter (diameter = 2 × radius) and draws a circle of radius 3 cm.
  • Faces, edges and vertices of prisms and pyramids Says a triangular prism has 5 faces, 9 edges and 6 vertices and a square pyramid has 5, 8 and 5.
  • Plot and read coordinates in the first quadrant Plots (3, 5) and says that the point is 3 across and 5 up.
  • Draw a polygon from its vertices Plots (1, 1), (4, 1), (4, 3), (1, 3) and joins them to see a rectangle.
  • Eight compass points Names the direction between north and east as north-east and plans a route using them.
  • Turns of 90°, 180°, 270° Faces north, turns 270° clockwise and says which way you face (west).
  • Translate a shape and describe the move Slides a triangle "left 2, up 3" and describes the move from start and end.
  • Reflect a point in a mirror line Reflects (2, 5) in a vertical mirror line at x = 4 to get (6, 5).
  • Reflect a shape in a horizontal or vertical mirror Reflects a whole polygon by reflecting each vertex.
  • Draw and read bar charts with a scale Draws a bar chart with a scale of 2 per square and reads a bar that ends between labels.
  • Double bar charts Compares boys and girls on a double bar chart ("5 more preferred swimming").
  • Draw and read line graphs Plots temperature at each hour and reads the value between two marks.
  • Describe the trend of a line graph Says "it rose until noon, then fell" and which period changed fastest.
  • Read and complete two-way tables Fills in a two-way table (class × favourite sport) and answers "how many girls chose football?".
  • Line plots with fractions of a unit Plots pencil lengths in ¼ cm on a line plot and finds the difference between the longest and shortest.
  • Pose a question and collect data Rewrites "Do you like sport?" as "Which sport do you like best?" and tallies answers.
  • Pick the graph that fits the data Chooses a line graph for temperature over a week and a bar chart for favourite fruits.
  • List all outcomes of an experiment Lists the 6 outcomes of a die and the 4 of two coins (HH, HT, TH, TT).
  • Equally likely or not Says a 3-colour spinner with unequal slices is not fair and why.
  • Design a fair or unfair game Re-slices a spinner so one player wins about twice as often, or makes it fair.
  • Multi-step problems with mixed operations and units Solves "3 packs of 250 g plus 1.5 kg, how many kg?" by converting first.
  • Bar models for "times as many" Draws a bar for "Tom has 3 times as many as Mia" and finds both amounts.
  • Bar models for fraction problems Draws 5 equal parts to show that 3/5 of a bar is 24 and finds the whole (40).
  • Work systematically to find all solutions Lists every 2-coin total from {1, 2, 5, 10} in an organised table or tree and knows none is missing.
  • Explain and critique a method Explains 36 × 5 as "half of 36 × 10" so a friend can follow it and says what is wrong in someone else's steps.
  • Conjectures and counterexamples Tests "all numbers ending in 5 are divisible by 10" and finds 15 as a counterexample.
