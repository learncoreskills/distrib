Mathematics · Grade 1

  • Syllabus

Skills

  • Count on within 120 from any number Counts forwards within 120 starting from any number, not only from 0 or 1. Example: starts at 67 and says "67, 68, 69, 70…".
  • Count back within 120 Counts backwards within 120 from any number, including across a tens boundary. Example: from 83 says "82, 81, 80, 79…".
  • Skip-count in 2s and 10s Counts in steps of 2 and 10 from 0 and from any start, forwards and back. Example: 30, 40, 50 and 14, 16, 18.
  • Skip-count in 5s (and 3s, introduce-only) Counts in 5s to 100 and sees the pattern in the ones digit; explores counting in 3s. Example: "five, ten, fifteen…" on the hundred-square.
  • Read and write numerals to 100 Reads and writes any number to 100 in digits, with digits the right way round. Example: writes "36", not "63".
  • Number words zero to twenty Reads and writes the number words from zero to twenty. Example: matches "fourteen" to 14.
  • Ordinals first to tenth Uses first to tenth, and on to 11th-20th, for position in a line or row. Example: "the cat is fourth in the queue", "the twelfth runner".
  • One more, one less, ten more, ten less Finds one or ten more or less than any number to 100. Example: ten more than 47 is 57.
  • Compare two numbers with <, > and = Compares two 2-digit numbers and records the result with a symbol. Example: 47 > 39.
  • Order and place numbers on a line or 100-square Orders numbers to 100 and places one between its neighbours on a number line. Example: puts 47 between 40 and 50.
  • Estimate a collection to 50 and check Estimates a collection up to 50 and checks by grouping in tens. Example: guesses 30 beans, finds 37.
  • Ten ones make one ten Bundles ten ones into one ten and knows they are the same amount. Example: 10 loose sticks become 1 bundle.
  • Whole tens, 10 to 90 Knows 10, 20 … 90 are 1 to 9 tens with 0 ones. Example: 60 is 6 tens.
  • Build a 2-digit number with tens and ones Builds any 2-digit number from blocks or bundles. Example: builds 43 with 4 tens and 3 ones.
  • Name a number from its blocks Reads the tens and ones in a picture and says the number. Example: 3 rods and 8 cubes is 38.
  • The value of each digit Says what each digit is worth and that digit order matters. Example: in 47 the 4 is 40, so 47 and 74 differ.
  • Bonds to 10 by heart Recalls every pair that makes 10 instantly. Example: 3 and ? make 10.
  • Swap addends Knows 3 + 8 = 8 + 3 and uses it to start from the bigger number. Example: 2 + 9 as 9 + 2.
  • Add three numbers using friendly pairs Adds three 1-digit numbers by spotting a pair that makes 10. Example: 4 + 8 + 6 = 18 by pairing 4 and 6.
  • Three meanings of subtraction Tells take away, difference and missing part apart. Example: "how many more red than blue" is a difference.
  • Related addition and subtraction facts Writes the fact family of a trio. Example: 3 + 4 = 7 gives 7 − 4 = 3.
  • Add a 1-digit number or tens to a 2-digit number Adds ones or whole tens using place value. Example: 34 + 5 = 39 and 34 + 20 = 54.
  • Subtract whole tens Subtracts a multiple of 10 from a multiple of 10. Example: 70 − 30 = 40.
  • Add and subtract within 100 by tens then ones Splits the second number into tens and ones and jumps along a line. Example: 34 + 25 as 34 + 20 + 5.
  • 2-digit ± 2-digit with regrouping Meets regrouping with tens-and-ones blocks, no formal column. Example: 34 + 28 as 5 tens and 12 ones.
  • Equal groups as repeated addition and × Writes equal groups as repeated addition and a multiplication. Example: 4 groups of 5 is 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 4 × 5.
  • Arrays and 3 × 5 = 5 × 3 Shows with an array that the order of a product can swap. Example: 3 rows of 5 is also 5 columns of 3.
  • Sharing vs grouping and ÷ Tells sharing from grouping, writes it with ÷, and links ÷ to ×. Example: 12 shared by 3 is 12 ÷ 3, and 12 ÷ 3 = 4 because 3 × 4 = 12.
  • Skip-count to the 2, 5 and 10 table facts Reaches table facts by skip-counting along arrays, never as a gate. Example: 4 × 5 by counting 5, 10, 15, 20.
  • Add and subtract within 10 fluently Recalls sums and differences within 10 instantly. Example: 7 − 4 = 3 and 6 + 3 = 9 without counting.
  • Count on within 20 without fingers Adds and subtracts within 20 by counting on or back in the head. Example: 9 + 3 by counting on 10, 11, 12.
  • Make ten to add Fills a ten, then adds the rest. Example: 8 + 5 as 8 + 2 + 3.
  • Doubles and halves Recalls doubles to 10 + 10 and halves even numbers to 20. Example: double 7 is 14, half of 14 is 7.
  • Near doubles Uses a double and one more or less. Example: 6 + 7 as double 6 and one more.
  • Subtract within 20 by thinking addition Subtracts by finding what to add. Example: 15 − 9, think 9 + 6 = 15.
  • Add or subtract 10 mentally Adds or subtracts 10 to or from any 2-digit number. Example: 10 less than 63 is 53.
  • Bridge ten when adding (47 + 8) Adds a 1-digit number to a 2-digit number across a ten. Example: 47 + 8 = 47 + 3 + 5 = 55.
  • Bridge ten when subtracting (52 − 7) Subtracts a 1-digit number across a ten. Example: 52 − 7 = 52 − 2 − 5 = 45.
  • Derive tens facts from number facts Uses a fact to 10 to know the tens fact. Example: 3 + 7 = 10 so 30 + 70 = 100.
  • 2-digit ± 2-digit mentally Meets splitting or compensating in the head, never gating. Example: 34 + 20 + 5, or 38 + 20 − 1.
  • Odd and even by pairing Pairs objects and sees whether one is left over. Example: 14 pairs off with none left, so it is even.
  • Odd and even on the 100-square and by last digit Sees odd and even stripes and decides from the last digit. Example: 38 ends in 8, so it is even.
  • An even number is a double Writes an even number as a double. Example: 14 = 7 + 7.
  • Equal and unequal shares Splits a shape into equal shares and rejects unequal splits. Example: folds a rectangle into two matching parts.
  • Halves, thirds and quarters by name Names shares of 2, 3 or 4, knowing equal shares need not look alike. Example: two different cuts of a square can both be quarters.
  • Read and write 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 Reads and writes unit fractions and matches them to pictures. Example: writes 1/4 for one of four parts.
  • Three quarters Shades and names 3/4 as three of four equal parts. Example: shades 3/4 of a rectangle cut four ways.
  • Two quarters equal one half Sees 2/4 and 1/2 are the same amount. Example: folds a half into two quarters.
  • Half of a set Shares a small set into two equal groups. Example: half of 8 is 4.
  • A third or quarter of a set Finds a third or quarter of a small set, then a non-unit share. Example: 1/4 of 12 is 3, and 3/4 of 12 is 9.
  • Count in halves and quarters Counts along a track in halves and quarters. Example: 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1, 1 1/4.
  • The equals sign means "the same as" Reads = as balance. Example: 3 + 4 = 5 + 2.
  • True or false equations Judges a statement true or false. Example: 7 = 8 − 1 is true.
  • Missing addend or result Finds the missing number in an addition or at the end. Example: 8 + ? = 11.
  • Missing start or subtrahend Finds the missing number at the start or in a subtraction. Example: ? − 3 = 5.
  • Extend a repeating pattern Continues and describes a repeating pattern. Example: red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue…
  • Number patterns and their rule Extends a number pattern and states the rule. Example: 2, 4, 6, 8 is "add 2".
  • Growing shape patterns Extends a growing shape pattern and says how it grows. Example: a staircase grows by one tile each step.
  • Order lengths directly Orders three objects by length. Example: pencil, crayon, rubber from longest.
  • Compare lengths using a third object Compares two objects that cannot sit side by side using string or a stick. Example: does the table fit the door?
  • Measure with units end to end Lays identical units with no gaps or overlaps. Example: the pencil is 6 cubes.
  • Estimate then measure in units (bridge to standard cm) Guesses a length in units and checks. Example: guesses 5 clips and finds 6.
  • Compare masses on a balance Says heavier, lighter or balances. Example: the apple is heavier than the pear.
  • Measure mass with uniform units Balances an object with identical units. Example: three cubes balance the apple.
  • Compare capacities by pouring Pours between containers to see which holds more. Example: the jug holds more than the cup.
  • Full, half full, empty and measurement words Uses full, half full, empty and the comparison vocabulary. Example: "the bottle is half full".
  • Read the hour Reads and sets o'clock on analogue and digital clocks. Example: 7 o'clock.
  • Read the half hour Reads and sets half past. Example: half past 3.
  • Match analogue and digital time Matches a clock face to its digital display and writes it. Example: 3:30 is half past 3.
  • Compare and order durations Says which takes longer and orders events by length. Example: eating lunch takes longer than washing hands.
  • Rough units of time Picks seconds, minutes, hours, days or weeks for an event. Example: brushing teeth takes minutes, a school day hours, a holiday weeks.
  • Days of the week in order Names the days in order, with before and after. Example: the day after Tuesday is Wednesday.
  • Months and seasons in order Names the months and seasons in order. Example: after spring comes summer.
  • Find a date and weekday on a calendar Reads a monthly calendar. Example: finds the third Friday.
  • Name coins and notes Names local coins and notes. Example: points to the 5-coin.
  • Know each coin's value Knows what each coin and note is worth and swaps equal values. Example: five 1-coins equal one 5-coin.
  • Count coins of one value Counts a row of same coins by skip-counting. Example: four 5-coins is 20.
  • Count mixed coins to 20 Counts a mixed collection to 20. Example: 5 + 5 + 5 + 2 = 17.
  • Pay for an item under 20 Picks coins that pay for an item. Example: three 5-coins and a 2-coin buy a 17 toy.
  • Is the money enough? Says whether a purse covers a price. Example: 12 is not enough for 15.
  • Name 2D shapes Names circle, triangle, square, rectangle, pentagon and hexagon. Example: "that is a hexagon".
  • Shapes in any size and orientation Knows a shape stays the same when turned or resized. Example: a tilted triangle is still a triangle.
  • What makes a triangle a triangle Says it is closed with 3 straight sides, and colour and size do not matter. Example: rejects a shape with a curved side.
  • Name 3D solids Names cube, cuboid, sphere, cylinder, cone and pyramid. Example: picks the cone from a bag.
  • Link solids to everyday objects Matches solids to things around us. Example: a can is a cylinder.
  • Build composite shapes Builds a bigger shape from others, in 2D and from 3D blocks. Example: a house from a square and a triangle, or a tower model from cubes and cuboids.
  • Copy a figure on a grid Copies a figure on square or dot paper. Example: copies a small boat.
  • Straight and curved lines Tells straight from curved lines. Example: a road bend is curved.
  • Draw a straight line with a ruler Joins two points with a straight line. Example: draws between two dots.
  • Left and right Uses left and right from own position. Example: "raise your left hand".
  • Steps forward and backward Describes a route as steps. Example: "three steps forward".
  • Whole, half and quarter turns Names whole, half and quarter turns. Example: a quarter turn right.
  • Program a floor robot path Combines steps and turns into a path. Example: "two steps forward, quarter turn right".
  • Find objects on a room plan Locates objects on a simple plan. Example: "the bin is by the door" on the plan.
  • Sort by one criterion and explain the rule Sorts by one property and names the rule. Example: red and not red.
  • Sort by two criteria in Venn or Carroll layout Sorts by two properties. Example: red and round in overlapping hoops.
  • Build a 1:1 pictogram or block graph Builds a graph with up to 3 categories. Example: stacks 5 red and 3 blue icons.
  • Read a graph: how many more or fewer Answers questions from a graph. Example: 5 red, 3 blue, so 2 more red.
  • Sort events: certain, possible, impossible Sorts everyday events into will, might and can't happen (non-gating). Example: "the sun will rise" is certain.
  • Predict, then check (non-gating) Predicts "sure, maybe or never" and checks by trying. Example: a bag of only blue counters can never give red.
  • Choose + or − Decides add or subtract for a story. Example: "3 flew away" means subtract.
  • The four story types Recognises add to, take from, put together and compare stories. Example: "how many more" is compare.
  • Unknown start, change or result Solves when any part is missing. Example: "There were __, 3 flew away, 5 are left."
  • Represent a story with objects and a drawing Shows a story with objects or a quick drawing. Example: draws 5 birds and crosses out 3.
  • Write the number sentence Writes a sentence for a story. Example: 8 − 3 = 5.
  • Explain how you found the answer Says how the answer was found. Example: "I counted back three from eight".
  • Check an answer another way Checks with a second method. Example: adds back to check a subtraction.
