Mathematics · Kindergarten

  • Syllabus

Skills

  • Count to 30 Says the number words in order from 1 up to 30 without skipping ("1, 2, 3 … 20 … 30").
  • One word per object Points at each object once, and answers "how many?" with the last number said ("1, 2, 3, so 3").
  • See it at a glance Says how many are there without counting: dots, dice and fingers to 5, then 6–10 as "5 and some".
  • Same count, moved around Knows 8 objects are still 8 when spread out, packed or in a circle, up to 20.
  • Zero means none Says 0 when there is nothing, and knows 0 is a number ("the basket is empty, so 0").
  • More, fewer, same Tells which of two groups has more, fewer or the same, by pairing items or counting.
  • Give exactly n Counts out exactly the number asked and stops there ("give the dog 6 bones").
  • Numerals 0–20 Matches a numeral to its quantity and finds it among others ("the card 12 goes with 12 dots").
  • One more, one less Says the next number, one more, or one less than any number to 20 without counting from 1 ("one more than 9 is 10").
  • Count on and back Starts from any number and counts up, or counts down to 0 ("start at 7: 7, 8, 9…"; "5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0").
  • Order 0–10, find the gap Puts numeral cards 0–10 in order and names the card missing on a number track ("0, 1, 2, _, 4").
  • Which number is bigger? Compares two numerals to 10 without counting objects ("8 is bigger than 5").
  • Parts of a number Splits a number into two parts: 5 is 2 and 3 ("5 = 4 + 1 = 3 + 2").
  • 1st to 5th Uses first to fifth and last for positions in a line, and tells "third" (place) from "3" (amount).
  • Write numerals Writes numerals the right way round with the right strokes, and picks the correctly formed one.
  • Count by 10s, 2s, 5s Chants by tens to 100, and starts 2s and 5s along a track, just for fun with no pass mark.
  • Estimate "about how many" Guesses "about 10" before counting a set and checks by counting.
  • Doubles to 5 + 5 Recalls doubles up to 5 + 5 ("double 4 is 8").
  • Halves of even numbers to 20 Shares an even number into two equal sets and says the half ("half of 8 is 4").
  • Quick facts within 10 Answers simple ± within 10 quickly with no penalty for slow answers ("4 and 3 — 7").
  • See how numbers to 10 are built Sees each number to 10 as a full part and an empty part, such as on a ten-frame ("6 is 5 and 1 more, with 4 spaces left").
  • Join stories: act out and put together Acts out "add to" and "put together" stories within 10 with objects ("3 ducks and 2 more come — 5 altogether").
  • Separate stories: take away and how many left Acts out "take from" stories within 10 and says what is left ("5 cookies, eat 2, 3 left").
  • Choose plus or minus for a story Says whether a story or picture needs joining or taking away ("they fly away, so minus").
  • Solve a story within 10 Solves a join or separate story with objects, fingers or a drawing ("draw 4 flowers, 3 more, count: 7").
  • Write a number sentence Writes or picks the number sentence that goes with a story ("3 + 2 = 5").
  • Find the partner that makes 10 Names the number that goes with another to make 10 ("7 and 3 make 10").
  • Facts within 5 Recalls ± facts with totals up to 5 ("2 + 3 = 5", "4 − 1 = 3").
  • First look at bridging into 11–20 Fills a ten-frame then adds the rest over 10 ("8 + 5: 8 and 2 make 10, 3 more is 13").
  • Share fairly between 2 or 3 Shares a set fairly and notices unequal shares ("6 cookies for 2 friends: 3 each"). No ÷ symbol.
  • Make equal groups Makes groups with the same number in each ("3 bags of 2"). No × symbol.
  • Equal and unequal parts Tells a fair split from an uneven one ("these two parts are not the same size").
  • Halves of shapes Folds or cuts a shape into 2 equal parts and names each one "a half".
  • Quarters of shapes Folds or cuts a shape into 4 equal parts and names a "quarter".
  • Half of a set Splits a small set in two equal parts and says "half of 8 is 4".
  • Make pairs with none left over Pairs objects to 10 and says whether any are left over ("6 makes 3 pairs, 7 leaves one over").
  • Even and odd by feel Sorts numbers to 10 into "pairs up" and "one left over" ("8 pairs up, 9 does not").
  • Continue an AB pattern Continues an AB pattern ("red-blue, red-blue, … then red").
  • Name the unit and fix a mistake Names the repeating unit and finds or fills what is wrong or missing ("the unit is clap-stomp, this one broke it").
  • ABB and ABC patterns Continues ABB and ABC patterns ("clap-stomp-stomp, clap-stomp-stomp").
  • Create your own pattern Builds a pattern with a chosen unit and explains it ("my pattern goes star-heart-heart").
  • Translate a pattern into another form Changes a pattern into another medium ("clap-stomp = red-blue").
  • Size words and which attribute to measure Uses big/small, long/short, heavy/light, full/empty and picks the right attribute ("measure length for a rope").
  • Compare two lengths Lines up the ends and says which is longer or shorter ("this pencil is longer").
  • Compare two weights Uses a balance or hands to say which is heavier or lighter ("the apple is heavier").
  • Compare two capacities Pours and says which holds more or less, full or empty ("the jug holds more than the cup").
  • Choose a tool Picks the tool that measures the attribute ("a ruler for length, a scale for weight").
  • Order 3–5 objects Puts 3–5 objects in order by length, weight or capacity and finds the one in the middle.
  • Order events with first, next, last Orders 3–5 pictures of a routine ("brush teeth, then wash, then bed").
  • Before, after, now, later Uses before, after, now and later for events ("lunch is before nap").
  • Day, night, morning and evening Says what happens in the day or at night, and in the morning or evening ("stars come out at night", "we eat breakfast in the morning").
  • Yesterday, today, tomorrow Names events as yesterday, today or tomorrow ("we went to the park yesterday").
  • Which takes longer Compares two activities ("brushing teeth is shorter than a bath").
  • Days of the week in order Says the seven days in order ("Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday…").
  • The day before and after Names the day after or before a given day ("after Tuesday is Wednesday").
  • Money buys things Knows that coins and notes buy things ("I give money and get the toy"). Local, non-gating.
  • Recognise local coins and pay with one Matches each coin to its picture and hands over the right coin for a toy; matches local notes to their pictures too. Local, non-gating.
  • Name circle, triangle, square Names the three basic flat shapes in any size or orientation ("this is a triangle").
  • Rectangle and hexagon in any orientation Recognises rectangles and hexagons, tilted or long and thin ("a tilted rectangle is still a rectangle").
  • Match identical shapes Matches same shapes of different sizes and colours ("both are triangles").
  • Count sides Counts the sides of a flat shape ("a triangle has 3 sides").
  • Corners and curved edges Tells corners from sides and straight edges from curved ones ("a circle has no corners").
  • Name solid shapes Names cube, sphere, cylinder and cone ("a ball is a sphere").
  • Flat or solid Says whether a shape is flat or solid ("a coin is flat, a ball is solid").
  • Roll or stack Finds which solids roll and which stack ("a cylinder can roll and stack").
  • Build bigger shapes from smaller ones Puts shapes together to make a new one ("two triangles make a square"); fits 3–5 pieces into an inset puzzle.
  • Spot shapes around us Finds shapes in the world ("a wheel is a circle, a box is a cuboid").
  • In, on, under, next to Places and finds things told with in, on, under, next to ("the ball is under the table").
  • Above, below, beside, between, behind, in front of Finds things from clues like "between the chair and the box" or "beside the lamp".
  • Copy an arrangement Rebuilds a block or tile layout from a model ("copy my tower").
  • Movement words Moves as told: up/down, forwards/backwards, towards/away ("walk forwards, then away").
  • Follow a short route Follows a route with a few steps ("forward 3, turn").
  • Give a short route Tells a friend the steps to reach a goal ("go forward 2, turn, forward 1").
  • Sort by one attribute Sorts objects by one attribute ("all the red ones").
  • Name the sorting rule Says the rule for a group ("these are all round").
  • Count and order the groups Counts each sorted group and puts them in order ("5 red, 3 blue"), and pairs matching items or things that go together ("cup and saucer").
  • Build and read a picture graph Builds a real-object or picture graph and says which has more ("more children like apples").
  • Math words in play Uses words such as more, fewer, same and equal in play ("I have more").
  • Say how you know Explains an answer ("I counted").
  • Enough for everyone? Matches items to children and says if there is enough ("5 cups, 6 children — one short").
  • Keep trying another way Tries again with a different way when a build or puzzle fails ("that fell, try a wider base").
