# Numbers syllabus — PK → G5 (+ G6 bridge) (proposal v3, challenges MATH-COMPETENCY-REFERENCE)

> **Three rules (set by the product owner, 2026-10-05):**
> 1. **Earliest-grade rule** — a skill belongs to the *earliest* grade at which *any* surveyed country
>    teaches it (not the consensus grade the reference uses today). **Confirmed 2026-10-05: a single-country
>    pull is fully core** ("grade 2 is grade 2" — no median cap, no extension flag); order inside a grade may
>    differ from any one country; earlier is more motivating.
> 2. **Entry to secondary = end of G5** — content that some countries teach in G6 (US G6, Singapore P6,
>    Japan G6, India 6) is pulled into G5. Only content taught *nowhere in primary* is not core.
> 3. **G6 (bridge)** — a non-gating level for number content taught in *no* primary syllabus but that
>    secondary assumes or opens with (England KS3 Y7, Singapore S1, US G7, Australia Y7, India 7, Japan JHS1).
>    Nothing in G6 blocks progress; it is a head start. Rows are tagged with source country + year.
>    **G6 also holds [USEFUL] skills (owner, 2026-10-05):** skills in *no* programme at all that still make a child
>    stronger at secondary maths — see §8b. They carry a one-line "why", not a country source.
>
> "Numbers" = reference topics NUM, PV, AS, MD, MM, FR, DEC, re-cut into 7 strands (the old split scatters
> concepts: rounding in PV+MM+DEC, percent in MM+DEC). Money, Measurement, Time, Algebra, Data stay separate.
>
> **Tags** — `(ID)` existing row kept in place. **[UP ←src]** pulled earlier by rule 1/2; source = country+year:
> ENG Y4 = England Year 4 (= G3 on the reference grid), SG P3, JP G4, US G5, IN 6.
> **[NEW]** missing today. **[FIX]** inconsistency. **[LOCAL]** culture-specific, non-gating.
> **[BRIDGE]** = a G6 row (rule 3): taught in no primary syllabus; non-gating head start.
>
> **Verification status (v3, 2026-10-05):** every [UP] source was re-checked against official documents where
> reachable; results, URLs and corrections are in **Appendix A (§11)**. Notes on the sources actually read:
> England NC (gov.uk, current), US CCSS (thecorestandards.org), Japan **2008** Teaching Guide translation (the
> 2017/2020 revision was not read), India NCERT *Ganita Prakash* 2024 (chapter lists via secondary sites),
> Singapore 2021 (MOE PDF returned 404 from this environment, so Singapore rows rest on secondary summaries
> that quote it — marked as such). Most [UP] moves also affect topics beyond Numbers (Measurement, Time, Data,
> Geometry) — the earliest-grade rule will move those as well.
>
> **v3 headline corrections:** (a) **integer ± is NOT a bridge item** — India Class 6 teaches it ("The Other Side
> of Zero"), so by rule 2 it is **G5**; (b) **ratio** is SG **P6** (not P5) and England **Y6** (= G5) already
> teaches it, so it needs no pull; (c) **2-digit × 2-digit and 3-digit × 2-digit** multiplication is JP G3 / SG P3
> (earlier than the proposal's G4), and **4-digit ÷ 1-digit** is SG P3 (proposal had G4); (d) SG P6 teaches
> **a:b:c ratios** and **find-the-whole-from-a-percentage** — both belong in G5, not G6.

**B. Pulled into G5 (rule 2)** — all taught in G6 somewhere: **fraction ÷ fraction** (US/JP/SG G6), **percentage
increase/decrease** (SG P6), **find the whole from a part and a percentage** (SG P6), **ratio incl. a:b:c, equivalent
ratios** (SG P6, JP G6, US G6; ENG Y6 already G5), **rates/unit rates, unit conversion by ratio** (US 6.RP.2–3;
speed JP G6), **HCF/LCM** (JP G5, US G6, IN 6), **prime factorisation, co-prime** (IN 6), **decimal × and ÷ decimal**
(JP/US G5), **order of operations with exponents** (US 6.EE.1), **absolute value, ordering, integer ± on a number
line** (US G6, IN 6 — **v3: integer ± moves here from bridge**).

**C. Gaps still missing in the reference:** "what percent is A of B"; find-the-whole (⅗ of ? = 30); fraction → decimal
by division (⅛, ⅓); find the percent/ratio as fraction; estimating to a suitable accuracy.

**D. Inconsistencies fixed:** G2 estimates to nearest 10 but rounding came at G3 (→ G2); order of operations only at
G5 (→ G3/G4); percent jumped in at G5 (→ seeded G4); decimals jumped G3→G4 (→ smooth G2–G4); G4 and G5 both
"simplify" (→ G4 simplest form, G5 only reuses it).

**E. G6 bridge ([BRIDGE], rule 3):** integer × ÷ and rational-number arithmetic, square roots, significant figures,
standard form intro, laws of indices intro, prime factors used for HCF/LCM/roots, percentages > 100% and reverse
percentages, proportion equations — taught in no primary syllabus (ENG KS3, SG S1, US G7–8, AU Y7, IN 7, JP JHS1).
Non-gating. Full list per strand below (each strand section ends with a **G6** line). **Removed from bridge (v3):**
integer ± (→ G5, IN 6), 3-part ratio and find-the-whole-from-% (→ G5, SG P6).

**F. Load check:** the rule moves ENG Y1/Y2 work (age 5–7) to K/G1. Treat grade as "introduced and practised
here", with fluency expected a term or a year later; K and G1 are the grades to watch for overload.
**v3 suggestion — *introduce-only* (I) vs *fluent* (F) by end of year:**
- **K** — F: count to 20 (and by tens to 100), numerals 0–20, ± within 10, bonds to 10, 1 more/less, halves of
  shapes. **I only:** counting to 100 by ones, skip-count 2s/5s (chanting, no facts), number words to 20,
  bonds/± 11–20, ×÷ with objects (grouping/sharing stories, no symbols), doubling/halving, quarters.
- **G1** — F: ± within 20 (no fingers), bonds to 10, 2-digit PV, ±10, doubles, 2/5/10 skip counts, halves/quarters.
  **I only:** 2-digit ± 2-digit with regrouping (fluent G2; G1 = tens-and-ones models, no formal column), tables
  2/5/10 as facts (arrays and skip-counting only; recall fluent G2), ÷ facts, thirds/¾/2⁄4 = ½ (concept +
  notation, no operations), derive-to-100 (3+7 → 30+70), odd/even, 3s skip-count, three-addend sums.
- **G2** (also heavy) — F: facts within 20, 3-digit ±, tables 2/3/4/5/10. **I only:** tables 6–9 (fluent G3),
  2-digit × 1-digit and 2-digit ÷ 1-digit written, numbers to 10,000, fractions beyond ½ ¼ ¾ (non-unit,
  equivalents, ± like denominators → fluent G3).
- App implication: mark I-rows as "explore", not as mastery gates, so a child is never blocked by them.

## 8b. G6 [USEFUL] — beyond-syllabus skills that help at secondary (curated)

Non-gating; **[USEFUL]** = in no programme, kept because it measurably helps at secondary. Chosen by the
orchestrator on 2026-10-05 from a longer candidate list; every row has a *why*. Struck candidates are listed at the end.

**Reasoning with numbers**
- **Why algorithms work** — explain column ×/÷ with the distributive law and an area model. *Why: removes
  "recipe maths"; the same idea becomes expanding brackets.*
- **Check three ways** — estimate, inverse, alternate method; decide which result to trust.
- **Error analysis** — find and classify mistakes in worked examples (sign, place value, carry, unit).
- **Prove or refute a number claim** — odd + odd, sum of 3 consecutive numbers, square of an odd, with a diagram or
  letters; counterexamples. *Why: first step of proof, bridge to ALG.*
- **Additive vs multiplicative reasoning** — "2 more" vs "2 times"; spot the misconception of adding the same amount
  to both parts of a ratio.

**Number sense at scale**
- **Fermi estimates** with powers of 10 and 1 s.f. *Why: magnitude sense, previews standard form.*
- **Instant recall:** powers of 2 to 2¹⁰, squares to 15², and √ placed between two integers (√50 is just over 7).
- **Number density** — between any two fractions or decimals there is another; there is no "next" decimal.
- **Number families** — natural, integer, rational; π and √2 as numbers that are not fractions (idea only).
- **Distance = |a − b|** on a number line, across zero. *Why: absolute value, later modulus and graphs.*

**Number theory**
- **Remainders and clocks** — modular thinking (day of week in 100 days; last digit of 7ⁿ), parity arguments.
- **Euclid's algorithm** for HCF; **number of factors** from the prime factorisation; sieve of Eratosthenes;
  triangular, square, Fibonacci and perfect numbers as pattern hunts. Binary counting only, to show what base 10 is.
- **Casting out nines** as a checking tool.

**Fractions and decimals**
- **Fractions as five things** — part-whole, number, quotient, ratio, operator (⅔ of); switch between them.
- **Recurring decimal ↔ fraction** (0.333… = ⅓, 0.999… = 1) and the terminating-decimal test.
- **Compare by cross-multiplication and by decimal conversion; complex fractions** (½ ÷ ¼, ¾ of ⅖).

**Computation habits**
- **Calculator and spreadsheet fluency** — brackets, Ans, ^, √; a small sheet to explore a pattern and to check a
  calculator answer against an estimate.

**Struck from the candidate list (and why)** — percent as multiplier / compound change (already a G6 row in RP, ENG
KS3 — kept there only) · divisibility by 7/11 (11 is already G5, IN 6; 7 has no payoff) · weighted average (DAT) ·
unit-conversion ratio chains and speed (MEA) · proportion table → straight line (POS/DAT) · writing number algorithms
in code (belongs to the computational-thinking subject) · bases other than binary (low payoff).
Missing-number equations and sequences with a rule stay in ALG.
